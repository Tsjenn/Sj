DESCRIPTION - paste this whole block into the description box
================================================================

Malacca, 1934. A twelve-year-old girl arrives from Guangdong holding the
hand of a stern black-and-white amah who tells the great house she is a
dead sister's child. Ho Mei Foong is given a mat by the kitchen door, a
name nobody in the family will trouble to learn, and a lifetime of
watching the people she serves.

She is not a servant's child. She is the mistress's own niece, hidden in
plain sight by a promise made at a deathbed, and she will spend seventy
years inside a house that never once calls her by her true name.

Then the war comes. When the Japanese reach Malacca in 1942, the family's
son does not come home from the screening, and Mei Foong is left with a
secret larger than the first one - one that will require her to give away
the only thing she has ever owned, and to spend the rest of her life
being called Auntie by her own daughter.

Kuala Lumpur, 2005. Su-Lin, an architect with a stalled career and a
called-off engagement, inherits a shophouse on Heeren Street from a woman
the family always described as an old servant. Inside, behind a loose
brick, she finds an English biscuit tin: a bundle of letters tied with
red thread, a wedding photograph with the bride's face folded out of
frame, one child's beaded slipper, and a birth paper that does not agree
with anything she has been told about who she is.

Spanning seven decades - the last years of British Malaya, the
occupation, the Emergency, and the night a country got its name back -
THE AMAH'S DAUGHTER is a novel about the women a household depends on
and never mentions: the ones who keep the accounts, keep the children
alive, and keep the secrets that hold everyone else's story together.

For readers of Lisa See, Min Jin Lee, and Kristin Hannah - a sweeping
family saga of duty, motherhood, and the long cost of a promise kept.

36 chapters. An illustration for each. One tin of letters that finally
gets read.

A note from the author: this is a work of fiction. The family, the house
and everyone in it are invented. The history around them - the dates, the
occupation, the Emergency, merdeka - is treated with care and without
invented statistics or borrowed real lives.

CATEGORIES - choose three
================================================================

In KDP's picker, choose "Literature & Fiction" in the Category
drop-down. NOT "History" - that is the non-fiction history shelf and
it will offer you centuries instead of genres.

  1.  Literature & Fiction > Genre Fiction > Historical Fiction
      (if a World War II or 20th Century placement is offered
       underneath it, take that one)
  2.  Literature & Fiction > Genre Fiction > Sagas
  3.  Literature & Fiction > World Literature > Asian
      (if not offered: Genre Fiction > Family Life)

You get three placements in total. Use Reset or the breadcrumb at the
top of the pop-up to come back up and take the next branch.

Do not spend all three inside Historical Fiction - they compete with
each other. See marketing/novel-categories-PASTE-THIS.txt for the
step-by-step.
