Written 19 September 2026. Zero revenue to date across twenty-plus
products. This is the plan that changes the channel, not the inventory.

## Why the channel is the problem, not the product

Every product so far was listed where nobody was looking: Gumroad has
no search traffic, itch.io has none for paid non-game-jam titles, and
KDP without ads is a lottery. Meanwhile the six Etsy listings prepared
on 22 August were never uploaded, and the twenty outreach messages were
never sent. Making a twenty-first product would land in the same place.

Fiverr is different in one way that matters: **buyers arrive with a
brief and a budget and search for someone to do the work.** No
audience is needed to get the first order. What is needed is a
listing that looks like the work is real — and this fleet has a
portfolio nobody else on the platform can show: four published books
with covers, eight games, two installable apps, three free tools, a
3D-animated video pipeline, financial models, and every piece of it
built with Claude Code, the exact skill Fiverr says is up 938%.

## How an order runs (the operating loop)

1. Buyer orders and answers the requirement questions Fiverr asks on
   your behalf (written in each gig file).
2. You paste the answers into this session. Nothing else — never the
   buyer's name or personal data, only the brief.
3. I build the deliverable, test it, and hand you the files.
4. You check it, then deliver on Fiverr. Revisions run the same loop.
5. After delivery: "If this was useful, a review helps more than
   anything." Reviews are the only ranking signal a new seller has.

Response time is a ranking factor. Fiverr's app on the iPad, with
notifications on, and reply within an hour during the day.

## Fiverr's rules that apply to us (Help Center, September 2026)

- AI is allowed in every category. Disclosure is required when a buyer
  asks or states a preference for non-AI work. Every delivery must be
  custom to that buyer — never reused or mass-produced. The gig
  descriptions say the work is built with Claude Code, so nobody is
  surprised.
- Gig title up to 80 characters, "I will" is mandatory; only the first
  50–59 show in search. Image 1280 × 769. Video 20–60 seconds, under
  50 MB. At least one image.
- Portfolio links to your own site are allowed; contact details or
  moving the deal off-platform gets the gig removed.
- Fiverr keeps 20% of every order.

## What I will not put in the listings

- "Chartered accountant", "accountant", "auditor" or "tax consultant"
  anywhere on the profile or gigs.
- Any number of past clients, orders, or earnings — there are none yet.
- Anyone's testimonial. Reviews come from real buyers after real orders.
- A promise about the buyer's sales, rankings or revenue.

## Honest odds

New Fiverr sellers with no reviews commonly wait weeks for the first
order, and many never get one. The things that move it: a portfolio
that is obviously real (ours is), a starter package priced to be an
easy first yes, reply speed, and the first two reviews. Expect the
first order to be small and the second to come from the first review.
If sixty days pass with impressions but no orders, the price or the
first image is wrong and we change them. If there are no impressions at
all, the category or tags are wrong and we change those. Either way the
signal exists, which is more than any product so far has produced.
