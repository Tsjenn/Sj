FULL NAME OF THE BOOK

   and What the Accounts Say

KDP has separate boxes. Split it like this:

   Title box     ->  The Silicon Ledger
   Subtitle box  ->  Who Really Owns Artificial Intelligence and What
                     the Accounts Say

Drop the dash in the subtitle box. Amazon's search results render
punctuation inconsistently and a stray em dash can look like an error.


================================================================
CATEGORIES — this is NOT a fiction book, so the path is different
================================================================

In the KDP picker, set the fiction/non-fiction switch to NON-FICTION.
Then use the picker's SEARCH box rather than scrolling.

Take these three:

  1.  Business & Money > Industries > Computers & Technology
      Search for: industries

  2.  Computers & Technology > Business Technology
      Search for: business technology

  3.  Business & Money > Economics
      Search for: economics

If your marketplace offers it, "Business & Money > Investing >
Analysis & Strategy" is a strong substitute for the third — but only
take it if you are comfortable that the book sits near investing
titles. It is NOT investment advice and says so throughout, which is a
selling point on that shelf rather than a problem.

WHY THIS SPREAD, AND WHAT TO AVOID

Avoid "Computers & Technology > AI & Machine Learning". It sounds
right and it is wrong for this book. That shelf is full of technical
and how-to titles, the readers there want to build things, and your AI
book already sits on it. Two of your own titles competing on one shelf
helps neither.

The reader for this book browses business and industry, not machine
learning. "Industries > Computers & Technology" is where books about
how an industry actually works get found.

AUTHOR BIO
================================================================

Tang Shiuan Jenn is a qualified chartered accountant. The Silicon
Ledger is a financial history of the AI and semiconductor industry,
written from the filings rather than the press releases.

================================================================

  sell any security, and no share-price predictions. Never market it
  as investment advice or imply that it is. That restraint is the
  product, not a limitation of it.
- Every figure in the book carries a date and a named source. Do not
  quote a figure from it in marketing copy without carrying the date
  across.
- Do not claim endorsement by, or a relationship with, any company
  named in the book.
- Do not quote reviews, rankings or sales figures the book does not
  have.
- The figures are snapshots as at August 2026 and will date. The book
  says so openly and tells the reader where to find current numbers.
