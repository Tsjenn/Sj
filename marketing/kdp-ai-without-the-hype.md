## Description (paste into the Amazon description box)

Everyone is using AI. Almost nobody is using it well.

You have typed a question into an AI assistant, received something impressive, and
quietly wondered two things: how much of this can I trust, and am I being left behind?

This book answers both — and it does something most books on the subject do not. It
contains no invented statistics, no fabricated case studies, no borrowed vendor
figures, and no promises about your income. Every claim in it is one a careful reader
could check.

Written by a chartered accountant for working professionals, it teaches the skill from
the ground up.

**WHAT YOU WILL LEARN**

• **How the machine actually works** — a five-minute mental model that predicts, in
advance, where AI will impress you and where it will quietly embarrass you.

• **The craft of prompting** — the six-part frame behind every good prompt, twelve
failure patterns with their fixes, and a reference library of forty reusable
professional prompts.

• **Beyond the chat box** — projects, your own documents, agents, tools, and assistants
that work directly in your files. What each is for, and when each is overkill.

• **How to make it reliable** — grounding, evaluation, and the Three-Check Rule: a
verification habit light enough that busy people actually keep it.

• **Sixteen industry playbooks** — finance, audit, tax, banking, insurance,
manufacturing, supply chain, retail, healthcare administration, legal, property,
education, marketing, HR, customer service, and the solo professional. Each with a
value map, ten concrete use cases, worked prompts, the sector's real risks, and a
30-day starting plan.

• **How to roll it out** — a 90-day adoption plan, a four-level training curriculum you
could teach next week, honest measurement, and a full sample AI policy you can adapt.

• **Risk, ethics and the law** — bias, ownership and copyright, the regulatory picture,
AI-enabled fraud and deepfakes, and a chapter on when NOT to use AI at all.

• **Your career** — what actually gets automated, the skills that get more valuable as
generation gets cheap, and a thirty-day plan to start on Monday.

**WHO IT IS FOR**

Accountants, lawyers, doctors and clinical administrators, engineers, teachers,
bankers, insurers, consultants, managers, and anyone who runs their own practice. It
assumes no technical background. It does assume you are busy and that being wrong has
consequences for you.

**WHY THIS ONE**

Most AI books are written by enthusiasts and sell you a future. This one is written for
people whose name goes on the work — and it is as clear about where to stop as it is
about where to start. One full chapter is devoted to the situations where the honest
professional answer is to close the laptop and do it yourself.

68 chapters. 8 parts. A diagram in every chapter, and tables throughout.

No miracle claims. No invented research. Just an honest, thorough, practical guide to
becoming the person in your organisation who uses this technology well — and knows
exactly where it stops.

## How to upload it

1. Go to kdp.amazon.com and sign in. Bookshelf → Create → Kindle eBook.
2. Paste the title, subtitle, author, description, 7 keywords and categories from
   this file. Title box: "AI Without the Hype" only.
3. Tick the AI-content disclosure. Say AI was used for text, and that you reviewed
   and edited it.
4. Upload `dist/AI-Without-The-Hype.epub` as the manuscript.
5. Upload `dist/AI-Without-The-Hype-cover.jpg` as the cover.
6. Use the KDP previewer and check: the title page, a part divider, one diagram, and
   one table-heavy chapter (Ch 18 or Ch 35 are the stress tests).
7. Set territories, royalty and price. Publish.

## Honesty notes for this title (do not remove)

- The book contains no invented statistics, studies, surveys, adoption rates, or ROI
  figures, and no fabricated case studies. Keep it that way in all marketing copy.
- Do not claim endorsement, partnership, or certification by any AI vendor.
- Do not claim the book guarantees income, savings, or job security.
- Chapter openings are second-person scenes ("It is half past four and...") or generic
  professional situations. They claim no specific experience for the author and name no
  real person, client, or firm.
- Every legal, regulatory, tax, clinical and professional passage carries a stated
  disclaimer that it is general information, that rules differ by jurisdiction and
  change, and that the reader must check their own position.
- Chapter 61 (regulation) deliberately names no statute, regulator or case anywhere, so
  the book stays true across jurisdictions and does not date.
- The previous working title, "Working With Intelligence", was changed because it sat
  close to an existing well-known AI title and because "AI" did not appear in it.
