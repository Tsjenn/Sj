WHY THIS ONE CAN BE GENUINELY SHOCKING

You asked for shocking. Good news: this book does not need a single
invented number to be shocking, and that matters — the moment you
exaggerate one figure, every other claim you make becomes a guess in
the reader's mind, and your whole positioning is that you do not make
things up.

Every line in the video is true, dated and sourced inside the book:

  - Nvidia lost about 589 billion US dollars of market value in one
    trading session on 27 January 2025 (CNBC and Bloomberg reported it
    the same day). Largest single-day loss of value by any company in
    market history.
  - Nothing physical changed that day. No fab closed, no product
    failed, headcount barely moved.
  - Anthropic closed a round at a 965 billion dollar valuation in May
    2026; OpenAI at 852 billion in March 2026. Neither publishes
    audited accounts.
  - A change in the assumed useful life of a server moves billions of
    reported profit without moving a cent of cash. This is ordinary
    accounting, done in public, in the notes.

That last one is the real hook for a finance audience. Most people have
never been told that profit is partly an assumption.


CAPTION

In one day, one company lost 589 billion dollars.

No factory closed. No product failed. Nothing physical changed. That is
the day I understood most people have no idea what a market
capitalisation actually is.

So I spent months reading the filings instead of the headlines. Two AI
companies with no audited accounts are worth about a trillion dollars
each. One assumption — how long a server lasts — moves billions of
reported profit without moving a cent of cash.

I am a chartered accountant. I wrote down what the accounts actually
say. 77 chapters. No price targets, no predictions, no advice.

WHAT NOT TO DO, EVEN THOUGH IT WOULD GET VIEWS

Do not say the market is about to crash. Do not say you know what
happens next. Do not put a price on any share. The book explicitly
refuses to do all three, and a video that does it would make the book
look like something it is not — and would be the one thing that could
actually damage you, because you are a chartered accountant talking in
public about listed companies.

"Here is a shocking fact that is true" outperforms "here is a
prediction" anyway. The 589 billion figure is shocking on its own. You
do not need to add anything to it.
