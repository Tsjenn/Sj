================================================================
WHO TO ASK — MAKE THIS LIST TONIGHT
================================================================

Twenty names. Not a market. People who already know you:

  - Every finance manager or controller you have worked with
  - Ex-colleagues who moved to other companies
  - Your university cohort — some of them run teams now
  - Anyone in your professional body's local circle
  - Your own company's suppliers, customers and auditors
  - Family friends who own a business

Rank them by how likely they are to reply, NOT by how big the company
is. Your first paying client will almost certainly be someone who
already trusts you. Start with the easy five.


================================================================
MESSAGE 1 — THE OPENER (send this to five people this week)
================================================================

Do not pitch. Ask a question you genuinely want the answer to.

  Hi [name],

  A question rather than a favour. Is your finance team using AI tools
  yet — and if they are, is there a written rule about what can be
  typed into them?

  I have been looking at this for a while and almost every team I ask
  says the same thing: people are already using it, and nobody has
  written anything down.

  Curious what it looks like on your side.

  [your name]

WHY IT WORKS: it is a real question, it costs them nothing to answer,
and the answer is almost always "yes, and no" — which opens the door
without you having pushed on it.

MESSAGE 3 — IF THEY SAY "SEND ME SOMETHING" OR GO QUIET
================================================================

  No rush on this at all.

  If it is easier, I can do a free thirty-minute version for your team
  — no policy drafting, just the part on what these tools get wrong
  and how to check them. It is genuinely useful on its own, and you
  can decide afterwards whether the full session is worth it.

  Either way, good to hear from you.

WHY A FREE THIRTY MINUTES: your problem is not price, it is that
nobody has seen you teach. One free session in front of a real team
gives you a reference, a testimonial you actually earned, and the
confidence that the material lands. Do this two or three times, then
stop giving it away.

================================================================
PRICING — YOUR DECISION, BUT HERE IS THE STRUCTURE
================================================================

I will not put a number on your work. What I can give you is the
shape most professional training is priced on, so you are not
inventing it under pressure:

  TIER 1  Free 30-minute taster        First 2-3 only. Then never again.
  TIER 2  Half day, up to 20 people    Your core offer.
  TIER 3  Full day with live workshop  Roughly double Tier 2.
  TIER 4  Half day + policy review
          + follow-up session at 30 days   Your premium.

THREE RULES

  1. Quote a fee, never an hourly rate. You are being paid for what
     the team leaves with, not for the hours you stand there.
  2. Price the second client higher than the first. You will be better
     and you will have a reference.
  3. Never discount. If they cannot afford Tier 2, sell Tier 2 with
     fewer people — do not sell the same thing for less.

Benchmark it against what a decent professional-body course costs in
Malaysia per delegate, multiplied by the number of delegates, and set
your fee somewhere sensible below that. You know that market. I do not.


================================================================
WHAT YOU ACTUALLY DELIVER — THE HALF-DAY AGENDA
================================================================

You already own all of this. It is in dist/AI-For-Finance-Teams.zip.

  0:00  What this is, and one honest warning
        The tool is most persuasive on the things it invented.

  0:15  A working mental model
        No maths. Why it predicts, why it is confident when wrong.

  0:45  Writing a prompt that works
        The six-part frame. Live: rewrite one of THEIR real tasks.

  1:30  Break

  1:45  Checking the output
        Figures, sources, reasoning. The one-page card.
        Live: hand out an answer with a planted error, let them find it.

  2:30  Where not to use it
        Ten situations. This is the part they remember.

  3:00  Draft your policy, together
        Nine questions on screen, filled in as a group.
        They leave with it written.

  3:45  What to do first
        Score two tasks against the five factors. Assign an owner.

  4:00  Close

THE PLANTED-ERROR EXERCISE IS THE ONE THAT SELLS THE NEXT BOOKING.
Give them a confident, fluent, wrong answer and watch how long it
takes the room to catch it. It usually takes a while. That moment is
worth more than an hour of your explaining.
