## The 18 points, corrected

**1. "Form an LLC this week."**
Malaysia does not have LLCs. Two steps, in order: (a) send the MIA email
in your Drafts and wait for the written reply — that is the one thing
that could turn a side business into a disciplinary matter; (b) register
a business name with SSM through EzBiz (a sole proprietorship, done
online, small annual fee — check the current fee on the SSM site). A Sdn
Bhd can wait until there is revenue to protect. Do not advertise
anything before (a) is in your inbox.

**2. "Pick a market where money already moves."**
Yours is chosen and it is the right one: finance teams and the owners of
SMEs that have them, in Johor and Singapore, plus the employers arriving
under the JS-SEZ. They already pay for training. They are already using
AI with no written rule. You already speak their language. Stay here. Do
not add a second market until this one has paid you three times.

**3. "Download Growman and scrape 25 accounts' followers."**
No. Scraping followers breaks Instagram's and LinkedIn's terms and gets
the account closed, and unsolicited marketing to individuals runs into
Malaysia's PDPA 2010 and Singapore's PDPA and Spam Control Act. The
enhanced version is slower and works: two lists, built by hand, in the
Prospects tab of the tracker.
- **Warm 25**: people who already know you (the kit lists where to find
  them). This list produces the first client.
- **Cold 25**: finance managers and SME owners you find on LinkedIn by
  searching job titles in your two cities. Business-to-business contact
  through a professional network is the legitimate route. This list
  produces clients four to ten.

**4. "Use AutoLead AI to send 100–200 automated DMs a day."**
Automated DMs are the fastest way to lose a LinkedIn account. The
honest volume is **five real messages a day, written by hand, five days
a week**. Twenty-five a week. The kit's Message 1 is a question, not a
pitch, which is why it gets replies. Log every one in the tracker the
moment you send it.

**5. "Create your offer before the first meeting."**
The thread's example offer — "10–20 extra sales calls a month" — is a
promise of an outcome you do not control. Yours must be a promise of a
deliverable you do control. Your offer, in one sentence:

> A half-day session for your finance team: how the tools work, how to
> check what they produce, where not to use them, and your team leaves
> with a one-page AI policy written and two real tasks scored and
> assigned.

Say what they leave with. Never say what will happen to their revenue.

**6. "100 DMs = 1 sales call; charge at least $5,000 a month."**
Those numbers are invented and the price is not mine to set. The kit's
working expectation is: ask twenty, expect around six replies, two
conversations and one yes. That is an expectation, not a statistic, and
after your first twenty the tracker replaces it with your real ratios.
Price: use the tier structure in the kit and the Offer Menu tab; put
your own numbers in the yellow cells.

**7. "Deliver by stealing top posts and rewriting them with ChatGPT."**
You do not need to. You own a four-hour agenda, a planted-error
exercise that sells the next booking, a policy builder, a scorecard and
a book. The delivery problem is solved. Only the selling problem is open.

**8. "Keep looping."**
Yes. After every session, Message 5 asks for one introduction. A
referral converts better than any cold message. This is the loop.

**9. "Build a personal brand from day one — post daily."**
Three posts a week on LinkedIn, not daily; ten are written in
marketing/LINKEDIN-POSTS.txt. Post them in the morning, then send that
day's five messages — people check the profile before they reply.
Never use "accountant", "tax consultant" or "auditor" in the profile
headline or posts; describe what you do, not the protected title.

**10. "Build AI systems, not AI services."**
This is the best point in the thread and the one that turns a workshop
fee into recurring income. The system you sell after the workshop:
- a monthly hour of "AI office hours" for the team,
- one new documented workflow each month (month-end checklist, invoice
  data extraction, board-pack first draft — whatever they scored
  highest in the room),
- a quarterly policy review as the tools change.
Bundle those as a retainer. It is Tier 5 in the Offer Menu, fee blank.

**11. "Collect testimonials aggressively."**
Collect them honestly. Only after a real session, only in the person's
own words, only with permission to use their name and firm. Two earned
lines beat twenty invented ones, and invented ones end the business.

**12. "Raise your rates."**
Kit rule: the second client pays more than the first, and you never
discount — you shrink the scope instead. Review the fee after every
third booking.

**13. "Learn one sales skill."**
The twenty-minute discovery call is below. It is questions, not a
pitch. Practise it once out loud before the first call.

**14. "Productise your service."**
Done — see the Offer Menu tab. Five named things a buyer can point at.

**15. "Replace low-value work with AI."**
Your low-value work is proposals, follow-ups and admin. I write those.
Your high-value work is the five messages and the calls. Guard it.

**16. "Turn each client into multiple streams."**
One finance team becomes: the workshop → the policy review → the
retainer → their auditors and suppliers by referral → the book as the
leave-behind → the same session for their Singapore office. The
tracker has a column for the next product per client.

**17. "Build an email list now."**
You have the beginnings: the free starter on Gumroad and two free tools
on the site. Add one habit: every workshop attendee is offered a short
monthly note on what changed in the tools. That list is the only
audience you own.

**18. "Distribution over perfection."**
The material is finished. From today the only measure that counts is
messages sent. The tracker's Daily Log has one row per day for that

## The twenty-minute discovery call

Ask, diagnose, explain. In that order. Do not open the one-pager until
minute fifteen.

1. **Open (1 min).** "Thanks for the time. I'd rather ask than pitch —
   is that alright? Tell me about the team: how many people, what they
   close each month."
2. **Ask (8 min).**
   - "Who on the team is already using AI tools, and for what?"
   - "Has anything gone wrong yet — a number that looked right and
     wasn't, something pasted in that shouldn't have been?"
   - "Is there anything written down about what can and can't go into
     these tools?"
   - "If the team could get four hours a month back, where would you
     want them?"
   - "Who else would need to sign off on a half-day session?"
3. **Diagnose (3 min).** Say back what you heard in three sentences.
   "So: three people using it, no written rule, one near miss on a
   figure, and you'd want the time back in month-end. Have I got that
   right?" Wait for the yes.
4. **Explain (5 min).** Now the offer, in their words. "The session does
   three things for exactly that: the checking method for figures, the
   ten situations to keep it out of, and your policy drafted in the
   room so nobody has to write it afterwards. Half a day, up to twenty
   people, and you leave with the policy and two tasks scored."
5. **Close (3 min).** One question, then silence: "Would it be useful to
   put a date on it?" If yes: date, room, headcount, invoice details.
   If not yet: "What would need to be true for it to be a yes?" Write
   the answer in the tracker and set a follow-up date.

Never quote a fee on the call unless asked. Send it in writing the
same day with the one-pager.

## The 30-day cadence

| Week | Daily | Once this week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill the Warm 25 in the tracker | Send the MIA email. Register with SSM. Post LinkedIn #1 and #2. |
| 2 | 5 × Message 1 to the warm list | Post #3, #4, #5. Reply to every response the same day. |
| 3 | 5 × Message 1 to the rest of warm + Message 3 follow-ups to week-2 silence | Run the first free 30-minute taster. Post #6, #7. |
| 4 | Fill and start the Cold 25, 5 a day | Second taster. Message 4 to ten people asking for a name. Post #8, #9, #10. |

Day 30 checkpoint, from the Dashboard tab: messages sent, reply rate,
calls, offers sent, yeses. If sent is under 60, the problem is sending.
If sent is over 60 and replies are under 10%, the problem is the
message — tell me and we rewrite it. If calls happened and nobody
bought, the problem is the offer or the price — tell me which objection
you heard.
