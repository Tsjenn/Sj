# Marketing playbook — how to actually get buyers

Digital templates are a proven market (Etsy alone sells hundreds of thousands of
them every month), but buyers have to *find* you. This playbook is ordered by
effort-to-payoff. Do Phase 1 fully before touching Phase 2.

---

## Phase 1 — Sell where buyers already search (week 1)

Your own store converts best, but it starts with zero traffic. Marketplaces have
the traffic built in — list there first and treat your store as the home base.

### Etsy (highest priority)
People type "budget spreadsheet", "invoice template", "habit tracker printable"
into Etsy every day with a credit card in hand.

1. Open a shop (listing fee ~$0.20/item; ~6.5% + payment fee per sale).
2. Create one listing per product **plus the bundle** — 4 listings.
3. Titles: front-load the exact search phrase, e.g.
   *"Budget Spreadsheet Excel & Google Sheets | Monthly Budget Template with
   Expense Tracker & Savings Goals | Instant Download"*.
4. Use all 13 tags per listing (e.g. `budget template`, `excel budget`,
   `google sheets budget`, `finance planner`, `money tracker`, `savings tracker`).
5. Images sell templates: make 5–8 listing images per product — clean screenshots
   of each sheet/page with a short benefit caption. Canva (free) is enough.

### Gumroad Discover
Fill in every field on your Gumroad products (description, tags, category, cover
image). Gumroad promotes complete listings in its own marketplace — free traffic.

---

## Phase 2 — Free evergreen traffic (weeks 2–8, ~3 hours/week)

### Pinterest (the #1 channel for printables & templates)
Pinterest is a search engine, and pins keep driving traffic for *years* — this is
what makes template income compound while you sleep.

1. Create a **business** account (free) named after your store.
2. Create boards: "Budgeting tips", "Freelance tools", "Goal setting & habits".
3. Make 3–5 pins per product in Canva (1000×1500 px). Pin styles that work:
   - Screenshot of the template + big text: *"The 5-minute monthly budget"*
   - Before/after: *"Where did my money go?" → "I know exactly where it goes"*
   - Checklist pins: *"7 things every freelance invoice needs"*
4. Every pin links to your Etsy listing or store.
5. Post 3–5 pins per week. Consistency beats volume; traffic typically starts
   building around weeks 4–8 and keeps growing.

### Short-form video (optional but powerful)
15–30 second screen recordings: "watch this spreadsheet build my budget in 60
seconds" posted to TikTok / Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts with the link in bio.
Template demos regularly go viral because the payoff is visible in seconds.

---

## Phase 3 — Compound (month 2+)

1. **Collect emails.** Add a free "mini" product on Gumroad ($0, e.g. a one-page
   weekly planner PDF cut from the Focus Planner). Free downloads collect emails;
   email every new template launch to past downloaders — they convert best.
2. **Ship one new template a month.** Each new product is a new set of Etsy/Pinterest
   search entry points and a reason to email your list. Ideas that fit this store:
   wedding budget, meal planner, subscription tracker, small-business bookkeeping,
   reading tracker, workout log.
3. **Seasonal peaks.** December–January ("new year, new budget/habits") and
   tax season are huge for these exact products — schedule extra pins for them.
4. **Raise prices with proof.** Once reviews come in on Etsy, test $12–$15 for the
   trackers; template buyers are not price-sensitive when reviews are strong.

---

## What to expect (honestly)

- **Weeks 1–4:** setup + first listings. A few sales from marketplace search.
- **Months 2–3:** Pinterest/Etsy SEO kicks in; steady trickle (often $50–$300/mo).
- **Months 4–12:** with monthly new products and consistent pinning, successful
  template shops commonly reach $500–$2,000/mo. Top shops go far higher, but they
  treat it like a real product line: reviews, images, new releases.

The single biggest predictor of success: **number of quality listings live**.
Ten good products beats one perfect product.
