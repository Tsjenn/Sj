What you just proved

You took one unchanging night and made it score anywhere from around 40 to the mid-90s by moving sliders. No sleep was harmed or improved. That is exactly what happens when the same night is scored by two different apps: different recipe, different number, same you.

And the "re-measure" button shows the second problem: some ingredients (stage estimates) are noisy guesses, so a recipe that leans on them produces a score that jumps around even when your sleep doesn't. If your score dropped seven points and you feel fine — this is usually why.

What's actually worth watching

Two of the ingredients need no guessing and no recipe: total sleep time and efficiency. They come from asleep-versus-awake detection, which consumer trackers genuinely do well. Watch those two lines, compare yourself only to your own recent average, and let the headline number be entertainment. The full reasoning is in our guide: What is a good sleep score?
