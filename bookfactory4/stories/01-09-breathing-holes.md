# January 9 — Breathing Holes

The pond in Wildhaven grew its first ice overnight — a lid so thin and clear you could see straight through it, like a window laid down flat.

Aquaphin surfaced at dawn in the middle, where the water stayed open, and looked around at the new silver edges of her home.

"Well," she said. "Hello, winter."

The ice was beautiful. It creaked and pinged and made little singing sounds as it grew. But Aquaphin swam a slow circle and noticed something the ice had not thought about.

The fish.

Down below, the minnows and the fat old carp were drifting sleepily near the bottom, the way fish do in winter. Fish sleep nearly all season, slow and dreamy. But even sleepy ponds need to breathe — fresh air has to reach the water somewhere, or the long winter gets stuffy for everyone under the lid.

"Not to worry," said Aquaphin. "This is a job I know."

She took a big breath and dove. Then she rose, gentle as a bubble, and nudged the thin ice with her nose — bump — until a neat round hole opened with a soft crick. She did it again near the reeds — bump — and again by the willow roots — bump — swimming from spot to spot, until the pond wore a necklace of little round windows.

The cold air kissed the water at every hole. The pond breathed in. Somewhere below, the old carp's whiskers wiggled, which is how carp say thank you in their sleep.

By evening, Aquaphin had made her rounds twice more, keeping each window open with a friendly bump of her nose. She was just checking the last one when the winter lantern came on across the park — and she stopped, and stared.

Every breathing hole had caught a piece of the amber light. All across the dark ice, the little windows glowed — one, two, three, four, five — like a string of small lanterns floating on the pond.

"Look at that," Aquaphin murmured. "The fish have night-lights."

Nocturnix sailed over on her wide, quiet wings and looked down at the glowing pond. "Neat work," she hooted softly, which from Nocturnix was a very big compliment.

Aquaphin floated on her back in the open middle of the pond, warm inside her own thick sleekness, and watched the stars come out above and the little lantern-holes glow below, until she was drifting more than floating.

The pond breathed slowly in, and slowly out. And Aquaphin rocked on the quiet water, eyes closing, cradled between one sky full of lights and another.
