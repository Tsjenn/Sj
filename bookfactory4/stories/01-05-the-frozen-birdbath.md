# January 5 — The Frozen Birdbath

The birdbath at the edge of the park had frozen solid in the night, and Cinderpup discovered it first, the way he discovered most things — nose-first.

*Bonk.* Ice.

He looked up. On the bare branch above, three winter birds sat fluffed into little feathery balls — a chickadee, a junco, a wren. They had come for their morning drink and found a mirror instead.

"Oh," said Cinderpup. "Oh, that won't do at all."

Now, Cinderpup had ember ears — soft and warm, glowing faintly orange at the tips, and warmest of all when his heart was busy caring about something. His heart was very busy now.

He put his front paws up on the rim of the birdbath and laid one ear gently against the ice, flat as a warm leaf.

Nothing happened. Then — *tick.* A tiny sound, like the ice clearing its throat.

Cinderpup stayed still. Being still was hard for a pup, especially with a tail that wanted to wag and kept having to be told *later, later.* But he thought warm thoughts — sunny windowsills, soup, the middle of a pile of sleeping friends — and his ears glowed a little brighter.

*Tick. Tick-tick. Creak.*

A dark circle of water bloomed in the middle of the white ice, then widened until it was wide enough. One thin ribbon of steam curled up into the cold.

The chickadee came down first, brave and bouncy. It sipped, tipped its head back to swallow, and sipped again. Then the junco, then the wren, and then — where had they all been hiding? — a whole soft crowd of them, taking turns around the warm circle like neighbors around a well.

Cinderpup sat in the snow with his ears cooling and his tail finally allowed to wag, sweeping a happy fan shape behind him.

He came back at midday, and again when the sky turned rosy. By the third visit the birds didn't even flutter when he trotted up. One bold chickadee perched right on his head, light as a leaf, and warmed its toes.

That night, Cinderpup curled up in his den and listened to the wind hum softly over the snow. Somewhere out there, the birds were tucked deep in the thick hedge, feathers over their feet, water in their tummies.

Tomorrow the birdbath would freeze again. Tomorrow he would warm it again. That was fine. That was the deal, and it was a good one.

Cinderpup sighed a long, happy sigh, his ears dimming to the softest ember glow, like two little nightlights.

And he drifted off to sleep, dreaming of small wings, warm water, and a chickadee's toes.
