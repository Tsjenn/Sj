# January 8 — Stained-Glass Snow

Glimmerwing woke when the winter lantern did.

That was her way. She was a moth-winged critter, soft as a whisper, and evenings were her favorite time — though on this particular evening, she was feeling a little plain.

All day the snow had shown off. It had sparkled like scattered sugar. It had glittered on every branch and gleamed on every roof. Even the icicles along Mossback's log shone like polished glass.

"Snow is very good at being beautiful," Glimmerwing sighed, sitting on a twig near the old oak. "And I am mostly good at being quiet."

She stretched one wing, the way you stretch when you're waking up — and something happened.

The lantern's amber light passed right through her wing, the way sunlight passes through a leaf. And down on the snow below, where her shadow should have been, there was no shadow at all.

There were colors.

Rose and gold and deep-sea green, soft violet and honey-orange, all glowing together on the white snow like a window made of candy. When Glimmerwing moved her wing, the colors moved. When she trembled it, they rippled like a quiet little river.

"Oh," she breathed. "Oh my."

She had always known her wings had patterns. She had never once thought to hold them up to the light.

Below, a door creaked open. Pebblit peeked out of his cave and stared at the glowing snow. Flufftail hung upside down from a branch to see it the right way up, which didn't help, but she enjoyed it anyway. Even Mossback, who was shy, came out as far as his front feet.

"Is it magic?" whispered Pebblit.

"It's Glimmerwing," said Flufftail.

"It's both," Mossback decided, and nobody argued.

So Glimmerwing did something she had never done before. She spread both wings wide in front of the lantern and moved them slow, slow, slower — and the colors drifted across the snow like a story with no words. Rose light slid over the drifts. Gold pooled in the paw prints. Green washed the old oak's roots like springtime visiting early, just to say hello.

One by one, her friends watched until their eyes grew heavy, and one by one they yawned and shuffled home through the colored snow to bed.

Glimmerwing folded her wings at last, and the snow turned white and still again, keeping the colors somewhere inside it like a secret.

She drifted to her cozy hollow, tucked her bright wings around her like a blanket. And she fell asleep glowing quietly, the way a stained-glass window does at night — resting, but never plain.
