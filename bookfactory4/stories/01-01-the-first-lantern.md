# January 1 — The First Lantern

Snow had fallen all day in Wildhaven, soft as sifted flour, and by evening every den and burrow wore a white hat. It was the first night of the new year, and it was Emberling's turn to light the winter lantern.

The winter lantern hung from the old oak at the middle of the park. It was round and made of thick amber glass, and when it was lit, everyone in Wildhaven could see its glow from their windows and know that all was well.

But tonight, the lantern's little door was frozen shut.

Emberling stood on tiptoe on the snowy branch and tugged. Nothing. She tugged again, with both paws. The ice held on tight.

"Hmm," said Emberling. She was a small critter, but she carried a warm flame for hair, and she had learned — slowly, the way anyone learns — that warm things work better than strong things, most of the time.

So she stopped tugging. She leaned her head close instead, until her flame nearly touched the frosty hinge, and she hummed a little tune while she waited. The tune was for her. The warmth was for the ice.

Drip. Drip-drip. The ice let go all at once, the little door swung open with a squeak, and Emberling laughed out loud in the quiet snow.

She lit the lantern. Amber light spilled across the white park like honey across a fresh bun.

One by one, windows answered. Flufftail's window glowed green-gold. Pebblit's cave shone silver. Down at the pond, Aquaphin surfaced just to look, and the light made a wobbly second lantern on the dark water. Far above, Nocturnix sailed one slow, silent circle around the oak — the night guardian's way of saying *well done* — before gliding home to her hollow.

Emberling sat on the branch a while, swinging her feet. The year was brand new. It didn't need anything big from anyone tonight — no adventures, no hurrying, no plans. It only needed one small lantern, lit patiently, and someone to notice how beautiful it was.

Snow began to fall again, slow and fat and unhurried, each flake turning amber as it passed the light.

Emberling climbed down, padded home along the row of glowing windows, and curled up in her warm den, where her flame settled low and sleepy like a candle deciding to rest.

Outside, the lantern glowed. The snow tucked Wildhaven in.

And everyone in the park, in their own warm somewhere, closed their eyes on the very first night of a long, kind year.
