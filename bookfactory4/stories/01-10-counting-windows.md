# January 10 — Counting Windows

Everyone in Wildhaven went to bed at night — everyone except Nocturnix.

Nocturnix was the night guardian. Her bedtime came at dawn, and her work was the gentle kind: gliding slow circles over the snowy park, keeping watch while everyone slept. And every night, before her own sleep, she did her favorite thing.

She counted the lit windows.

From the top of the old oak she could see them all, glowing gold against the blue snow. "One," she hooted softly — that was Flufftail's window, high in the maple. "Two" — Pebblit's cave, shining silver around the edges of his door. "Three" — the warm flicker of Emberling's den, which never quite went out, because Emberling herself was the fire.

"Four" — Cinderpup's window, where a small tail was still wagging in dreams. "Five" — Mossback's log, lit faint and green as a firefly being shy. "Six" — Bubbletide's bubble, drifting gently above the reeds with a glow inside like a night-light that floats.

One by one, as the night grew deeper, the windows went dark. That was how Nocturnix knew her park was falling asleep properly — each window winking out was another friend tucked in safe. Dark windows, full hearts. She counted those too.

But tonight, one window stayed lit.

It was Cinderpup's. Long after every other light had gone out, his little square of gold still glowed against the snow.

Nocturnix glided down and landed softly on the sill. Inside, Cinderpup was sitting up in his blanket, ember ears drooping, eyes wide.

"Can't sleep?" she asked through the frosty glass.

"I keep thinking the night is very big," said Cinderpup in a small voice.

"It is," said Nocturnix. "That's why I watch it for you. The night is big, but it's mostly full of soft things. Snow. Stars. Me."

"You'll be there the whole time?"

"The whole time. It's my favorite job. And when you wake up, it will be morning, and it will be my bedtime, and you can watch the day for me."

Cinderpup liked that very much. He turned around three times, curled into his blanket, and let his ears dim down to a cozy glow. His window went dark — the last one.

"All windows counted," Nocturnix announced to the quiet park. "All friends asleep."

She kept her slow circles going while the stars wheeled by, and when the sky finally turned the color of a peach, she sailed home to her hollow in the old oak.

The morning light came up soft over the snow, and the day began to watch over Wildhaven, just as promised. And Nocturnix tucked her head beneath her wing, closed her big amber eyes, and slept.
