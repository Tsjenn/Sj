# January 7 — The Snow-Pebble

The snow in Wildhaven was the good kind that morning — the kind that squeaks when you step and sticks when you squeeze — and Pebblit had a wonderful idea.

He would build a snow-pebble. A snow critter, round and sturdy, shaped exactly like himself.

He rolled a fat snowball for the body and patted it smooth with his small stone paws. He rolled a littler one for the head. He found two dark seeds for eyes and a curl of bark for a smile, and he stepped back to look.

Something was missing.

Pebblit tilted his head one way. He tilted it the other. The snow-pebble was round. It was sturdy. It had a very friendly smile. But it did not look like Pebblit — not quite, not yet.

Flufftail bounded past with snow on her whiskers. "Maybe a tail?" she offered, and swished her big fluffy one. But Pebblit didn't have a tail, so the snow-pebble couldn't either.

Cinderpup trotted by next. "Ears?" he suggested, his warm ember ears glowing. But Pebblit's ears were only tiny bumps, and tiny bumps of snow just looked like snow.

Pebblit sat down beside his almost-twin and thought. He thought so hard that he reached up, the way he always did when he was thinking, and tapped the little pebble cap on top of his head.

Tap, tap, tap.

"Oh," said Pebblit softly. "Oh, of course."

He hurried to his cave, where he kept a small shelf of favorite pebbles — smooth ones, speckled ones, one shaped like a heart. He chose the roundest, kindest pebble of all, carried it out with both paws, and set it gently on the snow-pebble's head.

A pebble cap. Just like his.

And there they were: two Pebblits in the white and quiet park, one warm-hearted and one snow-hearted, both wearing their caps, both smiling their bark-curl smiles.

"There you are," said Pebblit. "There you are at last."

He stayed beside his snow friend while the sky turned pink, then plum, then deep winter blue. The lantern on the old oak came on across the park, and its amber light rested on both little caps at once, as if it couldn't tell which Pebblit was which.

"Goodnight," Pebblit whispered. "Stay as long as the cold lets you. I'll be right inside."

He padded to his cave and burrowed into his mossy bed, cozy as a stone in the sun.

Outside, the snow-pebble kept its small, patient watch under the amber light. And Pebblit fell asleep smiling, because tomorrow morning, first thing, there would be a friend in the yard already waiting.
