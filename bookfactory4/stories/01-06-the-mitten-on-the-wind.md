# January 6 — The Mitten on the Wind

High above Wildhaven, where the wind runs in long smooth rivers, Zephyrix was taking her evening glide when something small and red came tumbling past her beak.

It was not a leaf. It was not a bird. It turned over twice, waved one woolly thumb, and kept on flying.

A mitten.

Zephyrix banked, swooped, and caught it gently in her claws. It was knitted from soft red wool, with a white snowflake stitched on the back. Somebody's mitten. Somebody, somewhere below, had one cold paw tonight.

"Well," said Zephyrix to the wind, "you found it. Now help me find who lost it."

She spiraled down through the falling dusk. She showed the mitten to Pebblit, who shook his pebble cap — far too big for him; a mitten that size would fit him like a sleeping bag. She showed Mossback, who admired the snowflake stitch but had never once worn a mitten, on account of the shell. Down at the pond, Aquaphin only laughed her splashy laugh. Fins don't get cold the way paws do.

Then Zephyrix remembered the big hill.

The big hill was where the sliding happened, and sliding was how things fell out of pockets and off of paws. She glided low along the long smooth track in the snow, and there, sure enough, halfway up, were small neat pawprints — and beside them, one mitten-shaped dent.

The pawprints led home. Zephyrix followed them all the way to a snug den at the meadow's edge, where a green-gold window glowed, and inside, Flufftail sat by her fire, frowning gently at one red mitten with no partner.

*Tap, tap.* Zephyrix knocked at the window with her beak and held up the traveler.

"My mitten!" cried Flufftail, flinging the window open. "It flew off on the hill and I thought it was gone for good!"

"It nearly was," said Zephyrix. "It got as high as the clouds. It has seen more of Wildhaven than most mittens ever will."

Flufftail set the two mittens together on the windowsill, side by side, thumbs touching, home.

Then she made warm honeyed tea, and the two of them sat while the fire made its small comfortable sounds and the night settled in around the den like a hen over her nest.

At last Zephyrix spread her cloud-soft wings and drifted up to her high roost, flying slow, the way you fly when the day is finished.

Below her, every window in Wildhaven glowed. The wind had gone quiet, carrying nothing now but snow.

And Zephyrix tucked her head beneath her wing and slept, dreaming of small red things finding their way home.
