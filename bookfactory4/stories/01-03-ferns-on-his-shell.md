# January 3 — Ferns on His Shell

Mossback woke slowly, the way he always did — first one eye, then the other, then a long turtle stretch that took about a hundred years.

Something felt different. The air was bright and cold and very still, and when he shuffled out toward the pond, Flufftail stopped mid-hop and stared.

"Mossback," she whispered. "Your shell."

Mossback pulled his head in halfway, just in case. "Is something wrong with it?"

"Wrong?" said Flufftail. "It's beautiful."

Overnight, frost had crept across his shell and left pictures there — silvery ferns, curling leaves, tiny feathered branches, all painted in ice. His little mushroom stood in the middle of it like a tree in a frozen garden.

The trouble with a shell, of course, is that you can never see your own. Mossback craned his neck this way and that, but the pictures stayed just out of sight.

"Will you tell me what it looks like?" he asked, very quietly, because asking was not easy for him.

So his friends did. Flufftail said the ferns swirled like water going happily downhill. Pebblit climbed a stone for a better view and reported one frosty shape exactly like a star. Aquaphin surfaced through the hole in the pond ice, looked for a long while, and said it looked the way singing sounds.

Mossback stood still in the cold morning and let the words settle over him, warm as any blanket. Shy critters don't always know it, but they can be carrying a whole garden and never see the flowers — and the flowers are there all the same.

He walked the long way around the pond that day, slow and proud, and everyone he passed found something new in the frost: a snail-swirl here, a feather there, a wave caught mid-curl.

By afternoon the sun leaned close and the ferns began to soften, and by evening they had melted back into plain, shining shell. Mossback didn't mind. Frost is like that. It visits; it doesn't stay.

"It will come back tonight," said Pebblit. "The cold paints while everyone's asleep."

"Then I'll sleep well," said Mossback, "and give it plenty of quiet to work."

He tucked himself into his den beneath the bank, where the ground was soft and the air smelled of moss and winter. He pulled in his legs, then his tail, then, last of all, his sleepy gray head.

Outside, the cold got out its tiny silver brushes and began, very gently, to paint.

And Mossback drifted off inside his own shell, snug as a letter in an envelope, dreaming of ferns unfurling slowly in the dark.
