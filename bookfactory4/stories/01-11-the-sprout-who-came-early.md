# January 11 — The Sprout Who Came Early

Emberling found it on her evening walk, in the snowy hollow behind the old oak: one small green sprout, poking up through the white.

"Oh, little one," she said, kneeling down. "You're early. Spring isn't for ages and ages."

The sprout didn't answer, because sprouts don't. But it shivered — or maybe the wind moved it — and its two tiny leaves were curled tight against the cold, like paws with no mittens.

Emberling's flame-hair fluttered. She knew about being small in a big cold world. And she knew one thing for certain: you don't scold somebody for arriving when they arrive. You just help them be warm where they are.

So she sat down in the snow beside the sprout, cupped her hands around it — not touching, just near — and let her gentle heat settle over it like a very small sunrise.

The tiny leaves uncurled. Just a little.

"There," said Emberling. "Better. But I can't sit here all winter, and you can't come home with me. Sprouts hate moving. Everyone knows that."

She thought. Her flame bent this way and that, the way it did when she was thinking hard.

Then she got to work. She patted the snow into a little round wall around the sprout, with a gap on the sunny side — for snow, funny as it sounds, is a blanket, and a den of snow keeps the wild wind out. She fetched a curl of dry leaf and leaned it overhead like a roof. Last of all, she found a smooth dark stone, warmed it between her hands until it held her heat the way bread holds its baking, and tucked it into the earth beside the sprout's roots.

"A warm stone to cuddle," she said. "I'll come trade it for a fresh one every evening. That's a promise, and I keep those."

The sprout stood a little straighter in its snug white den. Its leaves loosened, easy and unafraid, like small green shoulders letting go.

"You rest now," Emberling told it. "Grow slow. Take all the winter you need. Spring will still be spring, whenever you get there."

She walked home under the amber lantern, stopping once to look back. The little snow den sat round and quiet in the hollow, holding its speck of green and its cozy stone, safe as a wrapped-up gift.

At home, Emberling curled into her own warm den, and her flame settled low, the way it always did at bedtime. Somewhere out in the snow, something small was sleeping soundly, warm all the way to its roots. And so, very soon, was she.
